** Age & passage                                                   :noexport:
:PROPERTIES:
:header-args:emacs-lisp: :tangle no
:END:

The password store is migrated from GPG to [[https://age-encryption.org][age]]: entries live in
=~/.passage/store/ENTRY.age=, decrypted either by =age-plugin-yubikey= (PIV slot,
PIN policy =once= / touch policy =cached=) or by the cold recovery identity. Plan and
rationale: =~/documents/org/zknotes/20260727094235-migration_pass_gpg_vers_age_passage.org=.

Three layers, loaded in this order:

1. =age.el= — the EPA equivalent: a file-name handler that decrypts =*.age=
   transparently, so anything doing =insert-file-contents= just works.
2. =passage.el= — the native age port of password-store.el/pass.el
   (=passage-copy=, =M-x passage=). Built from git in =nix-config/home/modules/emacs.nix=.
3. a compat shim so the =password-store-*= commands and =M-x pass= that are already
   in muscle memory keep working against the =.age= store.

*** age.el

#+begin_src emacs-lisp
  (use-package age
    :demand t
    :custom
    ;; rage has pinentry support; upstream age deliberately has none. nixpkgs
    ;; patches age-program to an absolute age path, so override it explicitly.
    (age-program (or (executable-find "rage") (executable-find "age")))
    (age-default-identity "~/.passage/identities")
    ;; A recipients *file*: age.el accepts a pubkey, a path, or a list of both.
    (age-default-recipient "~/.passage/store/.age-recipients")
    :config
    (age-file-enable))
#+end_src

*** passage.el

=auth-source-passage-filename= must be set before the package loads: passage.el
computes its =auto-mode-alist= entry from =(passage-store-dir)= at load time.

#+begin_src emacs-lisp
  (use-package passage
    :demand t
    :custom
    (auth-source-passage-filename "~/.passage/store")
    :config
    (setq passage-store-executable (executable-find "passage")))
#+end_src

passage.el owns the =<store>/.*\.age= entry in =auto-mode-alist= (=passage-view-mode=
masks the password and renders otp headers). The shim below therefore does *not*
register a competing =pass-view-mode= entry for the same regexp.

Its =auth-source= backend is deliberately *not* enabled: =auth-sources= is
restricted to =~/.authinfo.gpg= (see [[*Authentication]]), and appending a second
backend would change resolution order for mu4e/msmtp. Enable it only if the mail
secrets are moved into the store:

#+begin_src emacs-lisp :tangle no
  ;; (auth-source-passage-enable)   ; appends `passage-store' to `auth-sources'
#+end_src

The =passage-store-otp-*= commands shell out to =passage otp=, a subcommand the
=passage= fork does not implement — they will fail. TOTP seeds live on the YubiKey
OATH applet (=ykman oath=) instead.

*** password-store.el / pass.el compat shim

Why a shim is needed at all: since password-store.el 20250618 the *read* path no
longer shells out to =pass=. =password-store-get= → =auth-source-pass-get= →
=insert-file-contents= on =ENTRY.gpg=, decrypted by EPA. So
=(setq password-store-executable "passage")= only redirects the *write* commands;
the =.gpg= extension stays hardcoded in five other places. These overrides swap it
for =.age=; age.el then handles the actual decryption.

#+begin_src emacs-lisp
  (with-eval-after-load 'password-store
    (setq password-store-executable (executable-find "passage")
          ;; password-store-dir prefers auth-source-pass-filename
          auth-source-pass-filename (expand-file-name "~/.passage/store"))

    (defun rb/auth-source-pass--read-entry (entry)
      "Read ENTRY.age; age.el's file handler decrypts it transparently."
      (with-temp-buffer
        (insert-file-contents
         (expand-file-name (concat entry ".age") auth-source-pass-filename))
        (buffer-substring-no-properties (point-min) (point-max))))
    (advice-add 'auth-source-pass--read-entry :override
                #'rb/auth-source-pass--read-entry)

    (defun rb/auth-source-pass-entries ()
      "List store entries by their .age files."
      (let ((store-dir (expand-file-name auth-source-pass-filename)))
        (mapcar (lambda (f) (file-name-sans-extension (file-relative-name f store-dir)))
                (directory-files-recursively store-dir "\\.age\\'"))))
    (advice-add 'auth-source-pass-entries :override #'rb/auth-source-pass-entries)

    (defun rb/password-store--entry-to-file (entry)
      (concat (expand-file-name entry (password-store-dir)) ".age"))
    (advice-add 'password-store--entry-to-file :override
                #'rb/password-store--entry-to-file)

    (defun rb/password-store-list (&optional subdir)
      (unless subdir (setq subdir ""))
      (let ((dir (expand-file-name subdir (password-store-dir))))
        (when (file-directory-p dir)
          (delete-dups
           (mapcar #'password-store--file-to-entry
                   (directory-files-recursively dir ".+\\.age\\'"))))))
    (advice-add 'password-store-list :override #'rb/password-store-list))
#+end_src

#+begin_src emacs-lisp
  (with-eval-after-load 'pass
    (defun rb/pass--tree (&optional subdir)
      (unless subdir (setq subdir ""))
      (let ((path (f-join (password-store-dir) subdir)))
        (if (f-directory? path)
            (unless (string= (f-filename subdir) ".git")
              (cons (f-filename path)
                    (delq nil (mapcar #'pass--tree (f-entries path)))))
          (when (and (equal (f-ext path) "age")
                     (not (backup-file-name-p path)))
            (password-store--file-to-entry path)))))
    (advice-add 'pass--tree :override #'rb/pass--tree)

    ;; pass-view opens ENTRY.gpg; point it at ENTRY.age. The buffer then gets
    ;; passage-view-mode from passage.el's auto-mode-alist entry.
    (defun rb/pass-view ()
      (interactive)
      (pass--with-closest-entry entry
        (find-file (concat (f-join (password-store-dir) entry) ".age"))))
    (advice-add 'pass-view :override #'rb/pass-view))
#+end_src

Known casualties of the shim: =password-store-init= (passage has no =init=;
recipients are managed by hand in =.age-recipients=) and pass.el's otp integration
(=pass-view--prepare-otp=). The overrides target internal functions, so a
=password-store.el= upgrade can silently break them — the canary is
=M-x password-store-copy= on a known entry after any rebuild.

* Fonts

  ~add-to-list~ is faster than ~set-frame-font~
  https://www.reddit.com/r/emacs/comments/f3ed3r/how_is_doom_emacs_so_damn_fast/

#+begin_src emacs-lisp
  ;; Enhanced font configuration for faster startup
  ;; Optimized version that avoids redundant font setting operations

  (defun my/setup-font ()
    "Configure font settings optimally for startup performance."
    (let ((font-spec "Hack Nerd Font Mono:pixelsize=18:weight=normal:slant=normal"))

      ;; Method 1: Set for current frame only (faster)
      ;; Only set if we're in a graphical environment
      (when (display-graphic-p)
        (set-face-attribute 'default nil :font font-spec))

      ;; Method 2: Add to default-frame-alist for future frames
      ;; This is more efficient than set-frame-font for startup
      (add-to-list 'default-frame-alist `(font . ,font-spec))

      ;; Alternative: Use custom-set-faces (even more efficient)
      ;; Uncomment if you prefer this approach:
      ;; (custom-set-faces
      ;;  `(default ((t (:font ,font-spec)))))
      ))

  ;; Performance optimizations
  (setq frame-inhibit-implied-resize t)  ; Prevents frame resize calculations
  (setq initial-major-mode 'fundamental-mode)  ; Faster than text-mode startup

  ;; Font fallback configuration (optional but recommended)
  (defvar my/font-candidates
    '("Hack Nerd Font Mono:pixelsize=18"
      "JetBrains Mono:pixelsize=18"
      "Fira Code:pixelsize=18"
      "Source Code Pro:pixelsize=18"
      "Consolas:pixelsize=18"
      "Monaco:pixelsize=18")
    "List of font candidates in order of preference.")

  (defun my/find-available-font ()
    "Find the first available font from candidates list."
    (cl-find-if (lambda (font)
                  (when (display-graphic-p)
                    (x-list-fonts font)))
                my/font-candidates))

  ;; Enhanced setup with font detection
  (defun my/setup-font-with-fallback ()
    "Setup font with automatic fallback detection."
    (when-let ((available-font (my/find-available-font)))
      (add-to-list 'default-frame-alist `(font . ,available-font))
      (when (display-graphic-p)
        (set-face-attribute 'default nil :font available-font))))

  ;; Choose your preferred setup method:
  ;; Option 1: Simple setup (use if you're sure the font exists)
  (my/setup-font)

  ;; Option 2: Setup with fallback detection (safer, slightly more overhead)
  ;; (my/setup-font-with-fallback)

  ;; Additional performance tweaks for font rendering
  (when (display-graphic-p)
    ;; Disable font scaling for better performance
    (setq inhibit-compacting-font-caches t)

    ;; Speed up font loading on some systems
    (setq use-default-font-for-symbols nil)

    ;; Reduce font lock overhead
    (setq font-lock-maximum-decoration t)
    (setq font-lock-maximum-size (* 1024 1024))) ; 1MB limit
#+end_src

** Filing documents by recipe

   [[file:mylibs/rb-filing.el][rb-filing]] moves a file where its name says it belongs: =TVA_07-2026_loopless.pdf=
   to =~/loopless/accounting/2026/tva=, creating the directory on the way.  The
   same recipes serve three entry points: =!= offers the move as its first guess
   (the usual guesses stay one =M-n= away), =R= defaults to the recipe directory,
   and =C-c f= does it in Lisp without a shell.  ~rb-filing-auto-mode~ applies them
   on a timer to ~rb-filing-watch-directories~; ~rb-filing-watch-mode~ is the file
   notification alternative.

   #+begin_src emacs-lisp
     (use-package rb-filing
       :ensure nil
       :load-path "mylibs"
       :demand t
       :config
       ;; set here rather than with the other dired options: it names a
       ;; function of this file, so it must not outlive it.
       (setq dired-dwim-target #'rb-filing-dwim-target-directories)
       (rb-filing-setup)
       (rb-filing-auto-mode 1)
       (with-eval-after-load 'dired
         (keymap-set dired-mode-map "C-c f" #'rb-filing-dired-do-file)))
   #+end_src

  (defmacro with-face (STR &rest PROPS)
    "Return STR propertized with PROPS."

  (defmacro esh-section (NAME ICON FORM &rest PROPS)
    "Build eshell section NAME with ICON prepended to evaled FORM with PROPS."
    `(setq ,NAME
           (lambda () (when ,FORM
                        (-> ,ICON
                            (concat esh-section-delim ,FORM)
                            (with-face ,@PROPS))))))

    "Accumulator for evaluating and concatenating esh-sections."

link — ~elfeed-entry-merge~ overwrites the stored link with the freshly
fetched one on every update, so rewriting at visit time only would not
survive.  ~rb/elfeed-rewrite-db-links~ patches entries that are already in
the database.

#+begin_src emacs-lisp
  (defvar rb/elfeed-url-rewrites
    '(("\\`https?://vitalik\\.ca/" . "https://vitalik.eth.limo/"))
    "Alist of (REGEXP . REPLACEMENT) applied to elfeed entry links.")

  (defun rb/elfeed-rewrite-url (url)
    "Return URL with `rb/elfeed-url-rewrites' applied."
    (when url
      (dolist (rule rb/elfeed-url-rewrites url)
        (setq url (replace-regexp-in-string (car rule) (cdr rule) url)))))

  (defun rb/elfeed-rewrite-entry-links (entries)
    "Rewrite the link of every entry in ENTRIES.  Return ENTRIES."
    (dolist (entry entries entries)
      (let* ((link (elfeed-entry-link entry))
             (new (rb/elfeed-rewrite-url link)))
        (unless (equal link new)
          (setf (elfeed-entry-link entry) new)))))

  (defun rb/elfeed-db-add-rewrite (args)
    ":filter-args advice for `elfeed-db-add'.
Entry ids are left alone so that entries keep matching their feed."
    (cons (rb/elfeed-rewrite-entry-links (car args)) (cdr args)))

  (defun rb/elfeed-rewrite-db-links ()
    "Apply `rb/elfeed-url-rewrites' to every entry already in the database."
    (interactive)
    (elfeed-db-ensure)
    (let ((count 0))
      (maphash (lambda (_id entry)
                 (let* ((link (elfeed-entry-link entry))
                        (new (rb/elfeed-rewrite-url link)))
                   (unless (equal link new)
                     (setf (elfeed-entry-link entry) new)
                     (setq count (1+ count)))))
               elfeed-db-entries)
      (unless (zerop count)
        (elfeed-db-save))
      (message "rb/elfeed-rewrite-db-links: rewrote %d link(s)" count)))

* Project terminals

Project-scoped =vterm= and =eat=, mirroring =projectile-run-vterm= /
=projectile-run-eat= but anchored on built-in =project.el=.  Each command
reuses the project's terminal buffer (named =*vterm PROJECT*= / =*eat
PROJECT*=), or starts a fresh one with a prefix argument.  Bound under the
=project-prefix-map= (=C-x p t= / =C-x p T=) and added to the project switch
menu.  =C-x p C= starts (or reuses) a Claude Code instance for the project,
named =*claude:PROJECT*= so each project keeps its own context.

#+begin_src emacs-lisp
  (defun rb--project-root-and-name ()
    "Return (ROOT . NAME) for the current project.
  Uses project.el, falling back to `default-directory' outside a project."
    (let ((root (if-let ((proj (project-current)))
                    (project-root proj)
                  default-directory)))
      (cons root (file-name-nondirectory (directory-file-name root)))))

  (defun rb--project-run-terminal (kind starter arg)
    "Open or reuse a KIND terminal rooted at the current project.
  STARTER creates a terminal in `default-directory'.  Reuse the project's
  existing KIND terminal unless ARG is non-nil, in which case always start a
  new one."
    (pcase-let ((`(,root . ,name) (rb--project-root-and-name)))
      (let ((bufname (format "*%s %s*" kind name)))
        (if-let ((buf (and (not arg) (get-buffer bufname))))
            (pop-to-buffer-same-window buf)
          (let ((default-directory root))
            (funcall starter))
          (rename-buffer bufname arg)))))

  (defun rb/project-vterm (&optional arg)
    "Open (or switch to) a vterm rooted at the current project.
  With prefix ARG, always start a new vterm."
    (interactive "P")
    (rb--project-run-terminal "vterm" #'vterm arg))

  (defun rb/project-eat (&optional arg)
    "Open (or switch to) an eat terminal rooted at the current project.
  With prefix ARG, always start a new eat terminal."
    (interactive "P")
    (rb--project-run-terminal "eat" #'eat arg))

  (declare-function claude-code-multi-new "claude-code-multi")

  (defun rb/project-claude (&optional arg)
    "Open (or switch to) a Claude Code instance rooted at the current project.
  The instance buffer is named after the project (*claude:NAME*), so each
  project keeps its own context.  With prefix ARG, always start a new one."
    (interactive "P")
    (pcase-let ((`(,root . ,name) (rb--project-root-and-name)))
      (let* ((bufname (format "*claude:%s*" name))
             (buf (and (not arg) (get-buffer bufname))))
        (if (and buf (get-buffer-process buf))
            (pop-to-buffer buf)
          (let ((default-directory root))
            (claude-code-multi-new name))))))

  (with-eval-after-load 'project
    (define-key project-prefix-map "t" #'rb/project-vterm)
    (define-key project-prefix-map "T" #'rb/project-eat)
    (define-key project-prefix-map "C" #'rb/project-claude)
    (when (listp project-switch-commands)
      (add-to-list 'project-switch-commands '(rb/project-vterm "Vterm") t)
      (add-to-list 'project-switch-commands '(rb/project-eat "Eat") t)
      (add-to-list 'project-switch-commands '(rb/project-claude "Claude") t)))
#+end_src

* Window management

  =ace-window= (above) and =windmove= handle /moving between/ windows.
  This section covers the other three problems: where a buffer gets
  /displayed/, how to /undo/ a layout that a package just wrecked, and
  how to keep several named layouts around.

  The engine here is ~display-buffer-alist~, not a package.  Anything
  that claims to "manage windows" is ultimately writing entries into
  it, so it is worth owning those entries directly.  =popper= is the
  one addition that pays for itself: it classifies transient buffers
  (compilation, REPLs, help, diagnostics) as popups, shows them in a
  dismissable bottom window, and lets one key cycle through them.

** Make display rules apply everywhere

   Without this, a plain ~C-x b~ bypasses ~display-buffer-alist~ and
   every rule below is silently ignored half the time.

   #+begin_src emacs-lisp :tangle no
     (setq switch-to-buffer-obey-display-actions t)

     ;; Don't split a window that is already small; prefer reusing one.
     (setq split-height-threshold 80
           split-width-threshold 160)
   #+end_src

** Winner: undo a window layout change


   ~winner-undo~ walks back through window configurations, which is the
   cheapest possible cure for "some package just took over my frame".

   Winner's own bindings are ~C-c <left>~ / ~C-c <right>~, which are
   already =windmove= here (see [[Windmove]]), so they are disabled and
   rebound under the ~C-x 4~ (other-window) prefix.

   With ~repeat-mode~ enabled, ~winner-repeat-map~ lets you keep pressing
   ~<left>~ / ~<right>~ after the first invocation; it is left off since
   it changes the behaviour of many other keys.

   #+begin_src emacs-lisp tangle no
     (use-package winner
       :ensure nil
       ;; `:demand' matters: with only `:bind', use-package would defer
       ;; loading until the key is pressed, and winner would have no
       ;; configuration history to undo by then.
       :demand t
       :init
       (setq winner-dont-bind-my-keys t)
       (setq winner-boring-buffers
             '("*Completions*" "*Help*" "*Apropos*" "*Buffer List*"
               "*Compile-Log*" "*Warnings*" "*Messages*"))
       :bind (("C-x 4 u" . winner-undo)
              ("C-x 4 U" . winner-redo))
       :config
       (winner-mode 1))
   #+end_src

** Popper: tame transient buffers

   Buffers matched by ~popper-reference-buffers~ stop competing for real
   estate: they open in a bottom window taking a third of the frame,
   ~C-`~ hides or restores the latest one, and ~M-`~ cycles through the
   rest.  ~C-M-`~ promotes or demotes the current buffer, so anything
   misclassified can be fixed in place.

   ~M-`~ is normally ~tmm-menubar~ and ~C-`~ does not exist in a
   terminal — under =emacs -nw= use ~M-x popper-toggle~ or pick other
   keys.

   #+begin_src emacs-lisp :tangle no
     (use-package popper
       :ensure t
       ;; Same reason as winner: popper has to be active before the
       ;; popups appear, otherwise it never classifies them.
       :demand t
       :bind (("C-`"   . popper-toggle)
              ("M-`"   . popper-cycle)
              ("C-M-`" . popper-toggle-type))
       :init
       (setq popper-reference-buffers
             '("\\*Messages\\*"
               "\\*Warnings\\*"
               "\\*Backtrace\\*"
               "\\*Compile-Log\\*"
               "\\*Async Shell Command\\*"
               "\\*vc-diff\\*"
               "Output\\*$"
               "\\*Shell Command Output\\*"
               help-mode
               helpful-mode

** Display rules for the rest

   Buffers that are not transient popups but still should not steal the
   editing window.  ~add-to-list~ rather than ~setq~, so re-evaluating
   this block does not drop the rules other sections add (Embark
   installs one of its own, see [[Embark]]).

     (dolist (rule
              '(;; Long-lived side panels, kept out of popper on purpose:
                ;; they are meant to stay visible next to the code.
                ("\\*gptel\\*\\|\\*Claude\\*"
                 (display-buffer-in-side-window)
                 (side . right)
                 (window-width . 0.4)
                 (slot . 0))
                ("\\*Dictionary\\*"
                 (display-buffer-in-side-window)
                 (side . right)
                 (window-width . 0.35)
                 (slot . 1))
                ;; Full-window applications: never split for these.
                ("\\*prodigy\\*"
                 (display-buffer-same-window))))
       (add-to-list 'display-buffer-alist rule))
   #+end_src

** Tab-bar: named layouts

   Built in since Emacs 27 and enough to replace =eyebrowse= or
   =perspective=: each tab holds its own window configuration.  The
   prefix is ~C-x t~ — ~C-x t 2~ new tab, ~C-x t 0~ close, ~C-x t o~
   next, ~C-x t RET~ select by name, ~C-x t r~ rename.

   The bar hides itself while there is only one tab, so it costs no
   screen space until it is used.

   #+begin_src emacs-lisp :tangle no
     (use-package tab-bar
       :ensure nil
       :init
       (setq tab-bar-show 1                     ; hide the bar with a single tab
             tab-bar-new-tab-choice "*scratch*"
             tab-bar-tab-hints t                ; number the tabs
             tab-bar-close-button-show nil
             tab-bar-format '(tab-bar-format-tabs tab-bar-separator))
       :config
       (tab-bar-mode 1))
   #+end_src

   ~tab-bar-history-mode~ offers per-tab layout undo on ~C-x t <left>~;
   it overlaps with =winner= above, so enable one or the other rather
   than both.
